🇯🇵 Nara with kids · Japan

Nara family guide

Nara is the easiest big day out in Japan for children: more than a thousand free-roaming deer, a bronze Buddha the height of a five-storey building and wide, flat park paths linking the main temples. It works as a day trip from Kyoto or Osaka, but staying one night lets you see the deer and lantern paths early, before the tour groups arrive.

Top things to do in Nara with kids

  1. Nara Park deer Ages 4+

    Buy a bundle of shika senbei crackers (about ¥200) from the official stalls, bow to a deer and many will bow back. Hand them over quickly, then show empty palms so the deer stop nudging.

  2. Todai-ji Great Buddha Hall Ages 4+

    One of the world's largest wooden buildings holds a bronze Buddha about 15 m tall. Behind it, children queue to crawl through a hole in a wooden pillar, said to be the size of the Buddha's nostril; adults pay ¥800 and children ¥400.

  3. Kasuga Taisha lantern paths Ages 5+

    The forest approach to this red-lacquered shrine is lined with around 2,000 moss-covered stone lanterns, and the outer grounds are free. Children can count lanterns and look for deer resting between them in the shade.

  4. Mount Wakakusa Ages 6+

    This grassy hill gives a wide view over the city and a picnic spot with deer, roughly 30-40 minutes' climb for school-age legs. It opens from mid-March to mid-December for a small fee of a few hundred yen.

  5. Naramachi and the mochi pounders Ages 4+

    Wander the old merchant lanes of Naramachi, then stop at Nakatanidou near Sanjo-dori, where staff pound green yomogi mochi at high speed with wooden mallets. A fresh piece costs around ¥200 and the show draws a cheering crowd.

Rainy-day and cold-day options

  • Naramachi Karakuri Toy Museum

    A free little house of traditional wooden mechanical toys that children are allowed to play with.

  • Nara National Museum

    Calm halls of Buddhist statues; collection galleries are free for under-18s, so a short visit costs little.

  • Naramachi Koshi-no-Ie

    A free reconstructed merchant townhouse where kids can explore tatami rooms, a tiny garden and steep wooden stairs.

A simple first day

Morning
Arrive early, buy deer crackers and walk through Nara Park to Todai-ji before the tour buses.
Afternoon
Have lunch near Kintetsu Nara, then follow the lantern path up to Kasuga Taisha.
Evening
Stroll Naramachi, watch the mochi pounding and eat an early dinner of somen or udon.

Getting around

Kintetsu Nara Station is a five-minute walk from the park, and trains from Kyoto or Osaka Namba take about 35-50 minutes. Everything central is walkable, with loop buses for tired legs; an ICOCA or Suica card works on all of them.

Food kids like

Try kakinoha-zushi (small sushi wrapped in persimmon leaves), chilled miwa somen noodles in summer and wobbly kuzu mochi with brown-sugar syrup. Lunch runs from about 11:30 to 14:00 and many Nara restaurants close by 20:00, so eat dinner early.

Safety notes

  • Deer are wild and can nip, butt or chase a child holding crackers, so let small children feed them with an adult and keep snacks and paper maps in a closed bag.
  • In autumn the stags are in rutting season and grumpier than usual, so keep a few metres from any male with antlers even though most are cut each October.
  • July and August are hot and very humid, so plan the park for early morning and carry water; vending machines with cold drinks stand almost everywhere.
