Philadelphia family guide

Philadelphia is where the United States was born, and it makes history easy for kids: you can stand next to the cracked Liberty Bell and walk through the room where the Declaration of Independence was signed. Then it switches to fun, with a walk-through giant heart at the Franklin Institute, a museum made for touching, America's first zoo and soft pretzels on every corner. The centre is compact and flat.

Top things to do in Philadelphia with kids

  1. Liberty Bell and Independence Hall Ages 5+

    See the cracked bell for free, then join a ranger tour of Independence Hall; free timed tickets are needed most of the year and cost US$1 each to reserve online.

  2. Franklin Institute Ages 4+

    Walk through a giant beating heart, see a real steam train and test science experiments; plan half a day and expect about US$25-35 per adult.

  3. Please Touch Museum Ages 1-7

    A huge hands-on museum in Fairmount Park with a mini supermarket, a water play area, an Alice in Wonderland maze and a restored 1908 carousel.

  4. Philadelphia Zoo Ages all ages

    America's first zoo, with Zoo360 mesh trails that let big cats and primates walk over visitors' heads; tickets are cheaper online for a set date.

  5. Franklin Square Ages 3-10

    A small Old City park with a carousel, Philadelphia-themed mini golf and a playground, open daily and best combined with the Liberty Bell.

Rainy-day and cold-day options

  • Academy of Natural Sciences

    Huge dinosaur skeletons, a dig pit and a live butterfly room on the Parkway.

  • Museum of the American Revolution

    Lively 1776 story with George Washington's war tent and a hands-on discovery centre.

  • Reading Terminal Market

    Covered food hall with Amish pretzels, ice cream and dozens of counters to graze.

A simple first day

Morning
See the Liberty Bell and tour Independence Hall before the school groups arrive.
Afternoon
Play at Franklin Square, then visit the Museum of the American Revolution.
Evening
Eat at Reading Terminal Market or share a cheesesteak in South Philly.

Getting around

Walk Old City and Center City, use SEPTA buses, the east-west subway line and trolleys, and take the Regional Rail straight from the airport.

Food kids like

Kids usually enjoy a cheesesteak, soft pretzels, Italian water ice in summer and Tastykake snack cakes; Reading Terminal Market is easiest for picky eaters, and dinner starts around 17:30.

Safety notes

  • Some neighbourhoods outside the centre, such as Kensington, are not for visitors, so stay in Center City, Old City and Fairmount with kids.
  • Summer can be hot and very humid at 30-35 °C, so plan indoor museums at midday and carry water.
  • Drivers turn fast on one-way streets, so cross at signals with the walk sign; city tap water is treated and safe.
