Innsbruck family guide

Innsbruck is a real city with a medieval old town, yet a cable car from the centre lifts you above 2,000 metres in about half an hour. That makes it easy with children: ski or sledge in winter, hike and swim in summer, and come back down for apple strudel by the river. Two Winter Olympics, a ski jump on the skyline and an Alpine zoo keep curious kids busy.

Stroller

The old town is flat and pedestrianised and trams are low-floor, but mountain paths and snowy streets call for a carrier or an all-terrain stroller.

Top things to do in Innsbruck with kids

  1. Nordkette cable cars Ages 4+

    Ride the Hungerburg funicular from Congress station, then cable cars to Seegrube (1,905 m) and Hafelekar (2,256 m) for huge views; adult returns cost roughly €40-55 and children pay about half.

  2. Alpenzoo Ages 3+

    Europe's highest-lying zoo shows Alpine animals only, from ibex and marmots to bears, wolves and bearded vultures, on a sloping site reached from the Hungerburg funicular.

  3. Old town and Golden Roof Ages 4+

    Look up at the Golden Roof's shining tiles, climb the City Tower stairs for a view over red roofs, and watch the colourful houses along the Inn river.

  4. Snow days or lake days Ages 4+

    In winter, family ski areas such as Patscherkofel and Muttereralm have ski schools and sledge runs; in summer, swap them for hikes with mountain huts and the Natterer See bathing lake.

  5. Swarovski Crystal Worlds Ages 4+

    A short shuttle ride east in Wattens, the giant grass head, the shimmering crystal chambers and the big outdoor play tower with climbing nets fill half a day.

Rainy-day and cold-day options

  • Audioversum Science Center

    Interactive exhibition about sound and hearing where kids test their ears and play with echoes.

  • Tirol Panorama

    A giant round painting of an 1809 battle next to the Bergisel ski jump, which you can visit afterwards.

  • Ambras Castle

    Renaissance castle with suits of armour, a cabinet of curiosities and a large park for drier moments.

A simple first day

Morning
Ride the Nordkette cable cars early, when the sky is clearest and queues short.
Afternoon
Stop at the Alpenzoo on the way down to meet the ibex and marmots.
Evening
Stroll the old town to the Golden Roof and share Kaiserschmarrn for dessert.

Getting around

The centre is walkable, IVB trams and buses cover the city, and free ski buses link hotels to the slopes for ski pass holders in winter.

Food kids like

Kids love Kaiserschmarrn (torn sweet pancake with apple sauce), cheese noodles called Kasspatzln and apple strudel; lunch is around 12:00 and many mountain huts serve hot food all afternoon.

What Innsbruck costs for a family

About €200-320 a day for a family of four excluding lodging; ski passes, rentals and lessons can double that in winter.

Safety notes

  • Mountain weather turns fast, so pack a warm layer and rain jacket even when the valley is sunny, and check the cable car webcams before going up.
  • Sun at 2,000 metres is strong on snow and in summer, so use high-factor sunscreen and sunglasses for children.
  • Trams run silently through the centre and bikes use marked lanes, so keep small children close at crossings in the old town.
