Cartagena family guide

Cartagena feels like a storybook port: thick stone walls built against pirates, flower-covered balconies, horse carriages and women selling fruit from bowls on their heads. Families pair slow walks in the old town with a giant Spanish fortress full of tunnels and a boat day to the coral islands. It is hot all year, so days start early and end with a breezy sunset on the walls.

Top things to do in Cartagena with kids

  1. Castillo San Felipe de Barajas Ages 5+

    Climb the huge ramps of Spain's largest colonial fortress and crawl through its dark tunnels; foreign adults pay about COP 35,000-45,000, so go early before the heat.

  2. Sunset on the city walls Ages 3+

    Walk the wide top of Las Murallas from the Clock Tower towards Baluarte de Santo Domingo, spotting cannons and sentry boxes as the sea breeze picks up.

  3. Rosario Islands boat day Ages 5+

    Boats leave Muelle La Bodeguita for clear water and snorkelling around Isla Grande; check that child life jackets are on board and expect a bumpy hour each way.

  4. Getsemaní street art Ages 4+

    Follow the murals, umbrellas and flags of Calle de la Sierpe, then join locals in Plaza de la Trinidad where street dancers perform most evenings.

  5. Totumo mud volcano Ages 6+

    About an hour away, kids float in warm, thick mud inside a small cone, then rinse off in the lagoon; local helpers expect small tips.

Rainy-day and cold-day options

  • Museo del Oro Zenú

    A small free museum of shiny pre-Columbian gold frogs, birds and jewellery.

  • Museo Naval del Caribe

    Ship models, cannons and pirate-era maps in a cool colonial building near the walls.

  • Choco Museo Cartagena

    A hands-on workshop where kids roast cacao and make their own chocolate bars.

A simple first day

Morning
Explore Castillo San Felipe de Barajas early while the ramps are shaded.
Afternoon
Rest through the heat, then stroll the old town balconies and plazas.
Evening
Watch the sunset from the city walls and eat in Plaza de la Trinidad.

Getting around

The walled city and Getsemaní are best on foot, and taxis are cheap for the fortress or Bocagrande if you agree the fare first. Island boats leave from Muelle La Bodeguita, with a small port fee paid in cash.

Food kids like

Kids usually love arepa de huevo, crispy patacones, coconut rice with fried fish and sweets from the Portal de los Dulces. Lunch is the main meal from 12 to 2 pm, and dinner starts late, around 7-9 pm.

Safety notes

  • Temperatures sit around 30-32°C with high humidity all year, so plan indoor time from noon to 3 pm and keep everyone drinking water.
  • Beach and street vendors can be persistent, so agree on prices for boats, massages or photos with palenqueras before saying yes.
  • Use insect repellent at dawn and dusk because mosquitoes carry dengue, and drink bottled or filtered water to be safe.
