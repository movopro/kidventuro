Seattle family guide

Seattle sits between saltwater and snowy volcanoes, and on clear days Mount Rainier floats over the skyline. Kids remember the fishmongers tossing salmon at Pike Place Market, the view from the Space Needle, a huge troll hiding under a bridge and the ferry ride across Puget Sound. Summers are dry and mild; the rest of the year brings drizzle, so good indoor museums matter.

Top things to do in Seattle with kids

  1. Pike Place Market Ages all ages

    Watch fishmongers throw whole salmon, see the first Starbucks queue and the bronze pig Rachel; go before 10 am on weekends to dodge the crowds.

  2. Space Needle Ages all ages

    Glass floors and tilting glass benches about 150 m up; timed tickets cost roughly US$35-45 for adults, and a combo with Chihuly Garden and Glass saves a little.

  3. Seattle Aquarium and Ocean Pavilion Ages all ages

    Touch pools with sea stars, sea otters and a shark-filled reef hall in the newer Ocean Pavilion on the rebuilt waterfront.

  4. Ferry to Bainbridge Island Ages all ages

    A 35-minute ride on a big green and white state ferry with skyline views; walk-on adults pay about US$10-11 and riders 18 and under travel free.

  5. Fremont Troll Ages all ages

    A giant concrete troll crushing a real Volkswagen Beetle under the Aurora Bridge, free to climb on, plus Gas Works Park nearby for kites and picnics.

Rainy-day and cold-day options

  • Museum of Flight

    Real jets, a space shuttle trainer and cockpits kids can sit in, south of downtown.

  • Seattle Children's Museum

    Pretend-play town for under-10s in the Seattle Center Armory building.

  • Museum of Pop Culture (MoPOP)

    Guitars, film monsters and a sound lab where kids can play real instruments.

A simple first day

Morning
Explore Pike Place Market and watch the fish throwing before the crowds arrive.
Afternoon
Walk down to the waterfront aquarium, then ride the Monorail to Seattle Center.
Evening
Go up the Space Needle near sunset to see the city lights and Mount Rainier.

Getting around

Link light rail runs from the airport to downtown and the university, buses and the 1962 Monorail cover the centre, and water taxis cross to West Seattle.

Food kids like

Kids usually like clam chowder, fish and chips, Pike Place mini doughnuts, teriyaki bowls and summer cherries; dinner starts early, around 17:30-18:30.

Safety notes

  • A few downtown blocks, especially around Third Avenue, can feel rough, so stick to busy streets and use the waterfront or buses with children.
  • Puget Sound is cold all year and beaches have tides and slippery rocks, so keep kids in sight and in shoes at the shore.
  • Steep hills and quiet electric buses make crossings tricky, so hold hands at lights; tap water is excellent and free everywhere.
