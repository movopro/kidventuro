Tenerife family guide

Tenerife is Europe's easy answer to a grey winter: about 20-24 °C by day from November to March, a four-hour flight from much of the continent, and a volcano to ride up. Families split their time between the sunny south coast around Los Cristianos and Costa Adeje, the green north with Loro Parque, and the moon-like crater around Mount Teide. Whales and dolphins live offshore all year.

Top things to do in Tenerife with kids

  1. Teide cable car Ages 4+

    An eight-minute ride climbs to 3,555 m among lava and craters; return tickets are about €42 for adults and €21 for ages 3-13, and wind or snow can stop it.

  2. Loro Parque Ages all ages

    Large zoo in Puerto de la Cruz with a real-ice penguin house, gorillas, parrots and sea lion shows; plan a full day and book online.

  3. Siam Park Ages 3+

    A Thai-themed water park in Costa Adeje with a wave pool, a lazy river and a separate kids' area; the water is heated, so it works in winter too.

  4. Whale and dolphin trip Ages 4+

    Pilot whales live between Tenerife and La Gomera all year; two- to three-hour boat trips from Los Cristianos or Puerto Colón cost roughly €30-60 per person.

  5. Playa de Las Teresitas Ages all ages

    A wide golden-sand beach near Santa Cruz protected by a breakwater, so the water stays calm for small swimmers.

Rainy-day and cold-day options

  • Museo de la Naturaleza y Arqueología

    Guanche mummies, volcano rocks and island animals in Santa Cruz.

  • Museo de la Ciencia y el Cosmos

    Hands-on science and a planetarium in La Laguna, with low entry prices.

  • Cueva del Viento

    Guided walk inside a long lava tube near Icod de los Vinos; book ahead and wear trainers.

A simple first day

Morning
Walk the promenade from Los Cristianos to Playa de las Américas and swim at a sheltered beach.
Afternoon
Take a two-hour whale and dolphin boat trip from the harbour.
Evening
Eat papas arrugadas by the sea and watch La Gomera's outline at sunset.

Getting around

A hire car is the simplest way to reach Teide and the north; TITSA buses link the main towns cheaply with a Ten+ travel card, and the south coast resorts are walkable.

Food kids like

Kids usually like papas arrugadas (wrinkly salted potatoes) with mild green mojo, grilled fish, Canary bananas and churros; dinner starts around 19:30 in resorts and later in local towns.

Safety notes

  • The sun is strong even in January and stronger still at Teide, so use high-factor sunscreen, hats and sunglasses all day.
  • Atlantic waves and currents are powerful on open beaches, so swim at guarded, sheltered beaches and follow the flags.
  • Teide is cold, windy and high, so bring warm layers, water and snacks, and come down if anyone feels dizzy or sick.
