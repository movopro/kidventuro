🇵🇷 San Juan with kids · Puerto Rico

San Juan family guide

San Juan mixes Caribbean beaches with real history: kids fly kites on the lawn of a 500-year-old fort, walk blue cobblestone streets and hear coquí frogs sing at night. Puerto Rico is a US territory, so the dollar is the currency, US citizens need no passport, and the El Yunque rainforest and a glowing bioluminescent bay are both easy day trips.

Top things to do in San Juan with kids

  1. Castillo San Felipe del Morro Ages 4+

    Explore cannon rooms, tunnels and sentry boxes, then fly a kite on the huge grass field outside; one USD 10 adult ticket also covers Castillo San Cristóbal, under 16 free.

  2. Old San Juan streets Ages 3+

    Walk the colourful lanes to Paseo de la Princesa and the pigeon park, Parque de las Palomas, and count the famous street cats on the way.

  3. El Yunque National Forest Ages 4+

    Climb Yokahú Tower and take a short waterfall trail in the only tropical rainforest in the US forest system; book the small vehicle entry reservation online in advance.

  4. Bioluminescent bay kayak Ages 6+

    At Laguna Grande in Fajardo, a guided night paddle makes the water sparkle with every stroke; tours cost about USD 55-80 each and work best on dark, moonless nights.

  5. Balneario El Escambrón Ages 3+

    This beach near Old San Juan has a reef-protected cove with calmer water, lifeguards and shade, good for younger swimmers and first snorkels.

Rainy-day and cold-day options

  • Museo del Niño de Carolina

    A big hands-on children's museum near the airport with play zones and science exhibits.

  • Museo de Arte de Puerto Rico

    Bright island art in a Santurce building with a sculpture garden to explore between showers.

  • Museo de las Américas

    Folk art, masks and carved saints inside the old Ballajá barracks in Old San Juan.

A simple first day

Morning
Explore El Morro early, while the stone walls are still cool.
Afternoon
Wander Old San Juan to Paseo de la Princesa and cool off with a piragua.
Evening
Swim at El Escambrón, then dinner in Condado and listen for coquí frogs.

Getting around

Old San Juan is best on foot, and short rides to Condado or Isla Verde are easy by Uber or taxi. Rent a car for El Yunque and Fajardo, each about 45-75 minutes away depending on traffic.

Food kids like

Try mofongo, crispy tostones, rice with pigeon peas and alcapurrias from the Piñones beach kiosks, plus a piragua from a cart. Lunch runs 12-2 pm and family dinners around 6-8 pm.

Safety notes

  • Rip currents are common on the open Atlantic beaches of Condado and Ocean Park, so swim at lifeguarded balnearios such as El Escambrón or Isla Verde.
  • The Caribbean sun is strong even on cloudy days, so use reef-safe sunscreen, hats and rash vests between 10 am and 3 pm.
  • Hurricane season runs June to November, peaking August to October, so buy flexible bookings then; tap water is treated, but bottled is handy after storms.
