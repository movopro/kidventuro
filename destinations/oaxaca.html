Oaxaca family guide

Oaxaca is a colourful, walkable colonial city where the air smells of roasting cacao and grilled tlayudas. Families climb the ancient Zapotec platforms of Monte Albán, paint wooden alebrije animals with village artisans and look for shapes in one of the widest tree trunks on Earth. Festivals such as Day of the Dead and the July Guelaguetza fill the streets with music, flowers and dancing.

Top things to do in Oaxaca with kids

  1. Monte Albán Ages 4+

    Explore a Zapotec city on a flattened mountaintop with a ball court and big stone platforms to climb; go early, as there is little shade, and under-13s enter free.

  2. Chocolate grinding on 20 de Noviembre Ages 3+

    At shops like Mayordomo, kids watch cacao, sugar and cinnamon ground into paste, then taste hot chocolate with soft egg bread.

  3. Alebrije workshop Ages 4+

    In San Martín Tilcajete or San Antonio Arrazola, families meet the carvers and paint their own small wooden creature to take home.

  4. Árbol del Tule Ages 3+

    This ancient cypress has a trunk over 40 metres around, and local children with flashlights show visitors elephant and lion shapes in the bark.

  5. Hierve el Agua Ages 6+

    Mineral springs flow over cliffs that look like frozen waterfalls, with shallow pools for a dip; it is a 1.5-2 hour drive and road blockades sometimes close access.

Rainy-day and cold-day options

  • Museo de las Culturas de Oaxaca

    Gold treasures from Monte Albán's Tomb 7 inside the former Santo Domingo convent.

  • Museo Textil de Oaxaca

    A small, often free museum of weaving, dyes and embroidery from Oaxacan villages.

  • Mercado 20 de Noviembre

    A covered market with a smoky grill hall where you pick meat and eat hot.

A simple first day

Morning
Walk the Andador to Santo Domingo church and peek at the cactus garden.
Afternoon
Watch chocolate being ground and lunch on tlayudas at 20 de Noviembre market.
Evening
Join families in the zócalo with balloon sellers and a cup of nieve.

Getting around

The historic centre is compact and best on foot, and taxis are cheap for short hops. Shuttles run to Monte Albán from the centre, and a hired driver is the easiest way to reach the valley villages.

Food kids like

Try tlayudas, stringy quesillo cheese, memelas, tamales in banana leaf, hot chocolate and fruit sorbet from the nieve stalls by the Basílica de la Soledad. The main meal is lunch, around 2-4 pm.

Safety notes

  • Oaxaca sits at about 1,550 m with strong sun, so use hats and sunscreen even when the air feels cool.
  • Tap water is not safe to drink, so stick to bottled or filtered water and choose busy street stalls with fast turnover.
  • Protests and road blockades happen from time to time, so check local news before long drives and leave buffer time before flights.
