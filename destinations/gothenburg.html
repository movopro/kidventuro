Gothenburg family guide

Gothenburg is Sweden's friendly west-coast city, built around canals and trams and only a ferry hop from smooth granite islands. Families come for Liseberg, Scandinavia's biggest amusement park, and Universeum, a science centre with a real rainforest and sharks. Between those, there are free animals in Slottsskogen park, giant cinnamon buns in Haga and summer swims from car-free islands in the southern archipelago.

Top things to do in Gothenburg with kids

  1. Liseberg Ages 3+

    Ride wooden and steel coasters, visit Rabbit Land for little ones and watch shows; the park opens roughly late April to September, with Halloween in autumn and a big Christmas market in winter.

  2. Universeum Ages 3+

    Walk through a steamy rainforest with free-flying birds and small animals, see sharks and rays in a huge aquarium tank and try hands-on science labs on several floors.

  3. Southern archipelago by ferry Ages 3+

    Take tram 11 to Saltholmen and a ferry included in your Västtrafik ticket to car-free Styrsö or Vrångö for rock pools, bike paths and summer swimming.

  4. Slottsskogen park Ages 3+

    This huge free park has seals, penguins, elk and a children's zoo in summer, plus the Plikta playground, one of the city's best.

  5. Paddan canal boat Ages 4+

    A low open boat cruises the 17th-century canals and under bridges so low that everyone has to duck; tours run from spring to autumn.

Rainy-day and cold-day options

  • Natural History Museum

    In Slottsskogen, with a famous stuffed blue whale and halls full of animals from around the world.

  • Museum of World Culture

    Changing exhibitions with family activities and creative workshops next to Universeum, free for children.

  • Valhallabadet

    Big public indoor swimming hall near Liseberg with a children's pool and family swim times.

A simple first day

Morning
Explore Universeum when it opens, before the queues build at the rainforest.
Afternoon
Walk to Haga for a giant cinnamon bun, then climb to Skansen Kronan for the view.
Evening
Spend the evening at Liseberg, when lights come on and lines get shorter.

Getting around

Gothenburg has a large tram network, and Västtrafik tickets bought in the To Go app cover trams, buses and the southern archipelago ferries.

Food kids like

Try köttbullar (meatballs) with lingonberry, a shrimp sandwich and the huge Hagabullen cinnamon bun in Haga; Swedes break for fika (coffee, juice and a pastry) mid-afternoon.

Safety notes

  • Archipelago rocks are slippery when wet and the sea is cold even in July, so swim at marked bathing spots and keep life jackets on small children in boats.
  • Trams have right of way and run quietly in narrow streets, so stop and look both ways at every tram track.
  • Liseberg is crowded on summer weekends, so give children a wristband or card with your phone number and agree a meeting point.
