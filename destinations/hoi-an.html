Hoi An family guide

Hoi An is a small, walkable river town of yellow merchant houses, tailors and silk lanterns that glow every evening along the Thu Bon River. Families cycle out to vegetable gardens and coconut-palm waterways, spin in round basket boats and finish at the beach a few kilometres away. The old town is car-free for much of the day, which makes it one of Vietnam's gentler places to travel with children.

Best ages

Every age enjoys the lantern boats and basket boats. Children of 7 and up can ride their own bike to Tra Que or the beach on quiet lanes, while younger ones sit in the child seats most rental bikes offer.

Stroller

The old town's lanes are flat but narrow, crowded in the evening and often without proper pavements. A light stroller works in the morning; for evenings, markets and boats a carrier is easier.

Top things to do in Hoi An with kids

  1. Ancient Town ticket walk Ages 5+

    The 120,000 VND ticket for visitors 15 and over covers entry to five sites such as old merchant houses, assembly halls and the restored Japanese Covered Bridge; younger children are usually free. Go before 10:00 when the lanes are calm and cool.

  2. Lantern boat on the Thu Bon River Ages 3+

    After dark, hire a small wooden boat for a 15-20 minute ride and let children float a paper lantern with a candle on the water. Expect about 150,000-250,000 VND per boat; agree the price before you board.

  3. Basket boats in Cam Thanh Ages 4+

    In the coconut-palm waterway of Cam Thanh, local rowers spin round bamboo basket boats and show children how to catch crabs and make grass toys. Rides cost roughly 100,000-200,000 VND per person; skip the loud music stretches by asking for a quieter route.

  4. Cycle to Tra Que vegetable village Ages 6+

    Ride about 3 km on flat lanes to this organic herb village, where farmers let children water plants with traditional shoulder-pole cans. Bikes are often free at homestays or about 30,000-50,000 VND a day.

  5. Lantern-making workshop Ages 6+

    In a 1-2 hour class children stretch coloured silk over a bamboo frame and take home their own folding lantern. Workshops in the old town cost about 150,000-300,000 VND per person and run in any weather.

Rainy-day and cold-day options

  • Family cooking class

    Many classes start with a market visit and let children roll fresh spring rolls and fold pancakes indoors.

  • Precious Heritage Museum

    A free gallery of large portraits of Vietnam's ethnic groups, with traditional costumes on display beside them.

  • Traditional Arts Performance House

    Short folk music and dance shows several times a day, included in the Ancient Town ticket.

A simple first day

Morning
Walk the Ancient Town early and visit the Japanese Covered Bridge before the heat.
Afternoon
Rest, then cycle to An Bang Beach for a swim and fresh coconuts.
Evening
Float a paper lantern from a boat and browse the night market across the river.

Getting around

Fly into Da Nang, about 45 minutes away by Grab or pre-booked car. In town, walk the old town and use bikes or Grab for Tra Que, Cam Thanh and An Bang Beach, all within about 5 km.

Food kids like

Try cao lầu noodles, white rose dumplings and a famous bánh mì; com gà, Hoi An chicken rice, is a safe favourite for fussy eaters. Locals eat lunch around 11:00-13:00, and evening street food along the river starts at dusk.

Safety notes

  • Motorbikes fill the streets outside car-free hours, so cross slowly and steadily without running, and hold children's hands on Tran Phu and the riverside.
  • Do not drink tap water; use bottled or filtered water, and from May to August plan outdoor time early because midday heat often passes 35 °C.
  • October and November bring heavy rain and river floods that can enter the old town, and the sea at An Bang is rough from October to February.
