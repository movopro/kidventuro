Strasbourg family guide

Strasbourg is a compact, flat city wrapped by the River Ill, with half-timbered Petite France, a pink sandstone cathedral and storks nesting in the parks. In December the whole old town turns into one of Europe's oldest Christmas markets, with gingerbread, carousels and a giant tree on Place Kléber. Outside the festive season it is calmer, cheaper and easy to explore on foot or by tram in two or three days.

Stroller

The centre is flat and trams are step-free, but Petite France has cobbles and market crowds in December make strollers slow; a carrier is easier in the evenings.

Top things to do in Strasbourg with kids

  1. Christkindelsmärik and Place Kléber tree Ages all ages

    Walk the market from Place Broglie to the cathedral square, ride a carousel and taste warm gingerbread; go before 4 pm on weekdays to avoid the heaviest crowds.

  2. Cathedral astronomical clock Ages 4+

    At 12:30 the apostles parade past Christ and a rooster flaps its wings; tickets for the show are a few euros and sell out on December weekends, so book online.

  3. Batorama boat trip Ages all ages

    A covered boat loops around the island past Petite France and the European Parliament, with an audio guide for children; about 70 minutes and €10-17 per person.

  4. Parc de l'Orangerie storks Ages all ages

    Free park with stork nests on rooftops and trees, a playground and rowing boats on the lake in warm months; storks are most visible from February to August.

  5. Le Vaisseau science centre Ages 3-12

    A hands-on science museum built for children, with water, building and animal zones; entry is about €12 per person or €40 for a family of four.

Rainy-day and cold-day options

  • Musée Zoologique

    Renovated natural history museum with big animal dioramas, free for under-18s.

  • Musée Alsacien

    Old Alsatian rooms, toys and costumes in a riverside house, quick to visit with kids.

  • Le Vaisseau

    Indoor science playground near the Rhine where children can spend a whole cold afternoon.

A simple first day

Morning
Walk from the cathedral to Petite France and watch the lock bridges turn for boats.
Afternoon
Catch the 12:30 clock show, then ride Batorama for a warm seated tour.
Evening
Wander the lit Christmas market streets and share a tarte flambée for dinner.

Getting around

The old town is walkable in 15 minutes end to end; CTS trams A to F link the station, Homme de Fer and the Rhine side with step-free stops.

Food kids like

Kids usually love tarte flambée (flammekueche), soft pretzels, bredele biscuits and the little brioche men called mannele; restaurants serve lunch 12-14 and dinner from 19.

What Strasbourg costs for a family

About €150-230 a day for a family of four, excluding lodging; December prices run higher.

Safety notes

  • December market crowds are dense, so agree a meeting point like the Kléber tree and keep phones and wallets zipped.
  • Trams are quiet and fast, and cyclists use many lanes, so teach kids to look both ways on every crossing.
  • Winter days are damp and near freezing, so pack hats, gloves and waterproof boots; tap water is safe to drink.
