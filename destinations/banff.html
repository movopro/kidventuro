🇨🇦 Banff with kids · Canada

Banff family guide

Banff is a small mountain town inside Canada's oldest national park, with elk wandering past the houses and peaks rising straight out of the streets. In summer families paddle canoes on turquoise lakes and walk canyon catwalks to waterfalls; in winter they ski, skate on frozen Lake Louise and soak in an outdoor hot pool while snow falls. It works well in both seasons, just pack very differently.

Top things to do in Banff with kids

  1. Banff Gondola Ages all ages

    An eight-minute ride up Sulphur Mountain to boardwalks along the ridge and an indoor centre; adult prices vary by date, roughly C$60-90, and under-6s ride free.

  2. Lake Louise and Moraine Lake Ages all ages

    Canoe on bright blue water in summer or skate on the frozen lake in winter; private cars cannot drive to Moraine Lake, so book the Parks Canada shuttle ahead.

  3. Johnston Canyon Ages 4+

    Metal catwalks bolted to the canyon walls lead about 1.1 km to the Lower Falls; in winter guided ice walks with spikes visit the frozen waterfalls.

  4. Banff Upper Hot Springs Ages all ages

    A hot outdoor pool at 39-40 °C with mountain views, open year round; a family swim costs roughly C$50-60 and swimsuits can be rented.

  5. Cave and Basin National Historic Site Ages 4+

    The warm spring cave where the national park began, with a short boardwalk, a film and tiny snails found nowhere else; youth 17 and under enter free.

Rainy-day and cold-day options

  • Banff Park Museum

    A wooden 1903 museum full of stuffed bears, birds and other park animals.

  • Whyte Museum of the Canadian Rockies

    Mountain history, old skis and climbing gear with family activity sheets.

  • Sulphur Mountain summit centre

    Indoor exhibits, a theatre and a café at the top of the gondola, warm in any weather.

A simple first day

Morning
Ride the Banff Gondola early and walk the boardwalk to Sanson Peak.
Afternoon
Visit Cave and Basin and walk the Bow River trail to Bow Falls.
Evening
Soak at the Upper Hot Springs, then eat a bison burger on Banff Avenue.

Getting around

Roam Transit buses link town, the gondola, Lake Louise and Canmore; a car is handy from Calgary airport, but summer parking at the lakes fills by early morning.

Food kids like

Kids usually enjoy bison burgers, Alberta beef, BeaverTails pastries, maple treats and Saskatoon berry pie; restaurants open early, with family dinners from about 17:30.

Safety notes

  • Stay at least 30 m from elk and 100 m from bears, carry bear spray on trails and make noise as you walk.
  • Winter often drops below -20 °C, so dress children in thermal layers and limit time outdoors when the wind picks up.
  • Lakes are glacier-cold even in August and mountain weather turns quickly, so use life jackets and pack rain gear; tap water is safe.
