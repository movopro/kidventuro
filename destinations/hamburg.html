🇩🇪 Hamburg with kids · Germany

Hamburg family guide

Hamburg is a working port city, and children feel it straight away: container ships the size of skyscrapers glide past beaches, ferries cost the price of a bus ticket, and red-brick warehouses stand on canals. Inside one of those warehouses is Miniatur Wunderland, the world's largest model railway, which alone justifies the trip. Add a famous zoo, huge parks and fish rolls on the pier.

Top things to do in Hamburg with kids

  1. Miniatur Wunderland Ages 3+

    Thousands of tiny trains, cars and planes run through model countries with day and night every 15 minutes; book a time slot online and plan at least three hours.

  2. Harbour by ferry line 62 Ages 3+

    From Landungsbrücken, the HADAG ferry 62 sails past Övelgönne beach and the container terminals to Finkenwerder for the price of a normal public transport ticket.

  3. Old Elbe Tunnel and Elbphilharmonie Plaza Ages 4+

    Walk under the river through the 1911 tiled tunnel, then ride the long curved escalator up to the free Elbphilharmonie Plaza for a 360-degree harbour view.

  4. Tierpark Hagenbeck Ages 3+

    A family-run zoo founded in 1907 with open enclosures, elephants, walruses and a separate Tropen-Aquarium with sharks, snakes and lemurs; take the U2 to Hagenbecks Tierpark.

  5. Planten un Blomen Ages 3+

    A central park with huge playgrounds, a water playground and free evening water-and-light fountain shows from May to September.

Rainy-day and cold-day options

  • International Maritime Museum

    Nine floors of ship models, uniforms and a ship simulator in an old Speicherstadt warehouse.

  • Chocoversum

    Chocolate museum tour where children mix and decorate their own bar to take home.

  • U-434 Submarine Museum

    Climb through a real Soviet-built submarine moored near the fish market, with narrow hatches and bunks.

A simple first day

Morning
Spend the morning at Miniatur Wunderland with a pre-booked early slot.
Afternoon
Walk through the Speicherstadt to Landungsbrücken and ride ferry 62 along the harbour.
Evening
Eat fish rolls on the pier and watch the lights come on at the Elbphilharmonie.

Getting around

U-Bahn, S-Bahn, buses and HADAG harbour ferries all use the same HVV tickets, which you can buy in the hvv switch app or at machines.

Food kids like

Kids usually love Franzbrötchen (flat cinnamon pastries), currywurst and Rote Grütze red berry pudding; brave eaters can try a Fischbrötchen with herring or fried fish at Landungsbrücken.

Safety notes

  • The Elbe has strong currents and big ship waves, so let children paddle only at the shoreline at Övelgönne and never swim there.
  • Red-paved bike lanes run along many pavements and cyclists move fast, so teach children to stay off the red strip.
  • St. Pauli and the Reeperbahn are lively at night and less suited to families after dark, though fine in daytime on the way to the harbour.
