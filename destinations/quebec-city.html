Quebec City family guide

Quebec City is a French-speaking walled town above the St Lawrence River that feels like a slice of old Europe in North America. In winter it is deep in snow, and from late January to mid-February the Winter Carnival brings Bonhomme the snowman, ice slides, night parades and canoe races on the frozen river. Summer is warm, green and full of street performers.

Top things to do in Quebec City with kids

  1. Winter Carnival (Carnaval de Québec) Ages all ages

    Late January to mid-February: meet Bonhomme at his ice palace, watch the night parades and snow sculptures; the Bonhomme effigy pass costs about C$20-25 (€12-16) for adults.

  2. Terrasse Dufferin toboggan slide Ages 4+

    A wooden slide in front of the Château Frontenac sends sleds racing down at high speed in winter; each ride costs a few dollars, and age rules for little ones are posted at the ticket booth.

  3. Montmorency Falls Ages all ages

    Waterfalls 30 m higher than Niagara, 15 minutes from town, with a cable car, a suspension bridge over the top and a frozen 'sugarloaf' of ice in winter.

  4. Village Vacances Valcartier Ages 4+

    Big snow-tubing park in winter and water park in summer, about 25 minutes north; the Hôtel de Glace ice hotel stands next door from January to March.

  5. Maple taffy on snow Ages all ages

    Warm maple syrup poured onto clean snow and rolled onto a stick; find it at the Carnival, sugar shacks in spring or shops on Rue du Petit-Champlain.

Rainy-day and cold-day options

  • Musée de la civilisation

    Hands-on family exhibitions about Quebec life, nature and history in the Lower Town.

  • Aquarium du Québec

    Polar bears, walruses and seals, with indoor halls for very cold or rainy days.

  • Morrin Centre

    An old jail turned library where kids can see historic cells on guided tours.

A simple first day

Morning
Walk the city walls and the Plains of Abraham, then try the Dufferin toboggan slide.
Afternoon
Ride the funicular to Petit-Champlain and warm up with hot chocolate and crêpes.
Evening
Join a Carnival night parade or watch Château Frontenac light up from the terrace.

Getting around

Old Quebec is walkable and the funicular links Upper and Lower Town; RTC buses and the Métrobus cover the city, but you need a car or shuttle for Montmorency and Valcartier.

Food kids like

Kids love poutine (fries with cheese curds and gravy), crêpes, maple taffy and BeaverTails pastries; tourtière meat pie is a winter classic, and dinner starts around 18:00.

Safety notes

  • Winter can drop to -20 °C with wind chill, so dress kids in thermal layers, waterproof mitts and face cover and go indoors to warm up often.
  • Pavements and stairs get icy, so wear boots with good grip and consider slip-on ice cleats for parents carrying children.
  • Cars drive on the right and turning traffic can be quick on slushy roads, so cross at lights; tap water is safe to drink.
