San Antonio family guide

San Antonio is an easy, friendly city for a first taste of Texas. Families float along the shaded River Walk, stand inside the Alamo, explore Spanish missions that are still working churches and fill up on breakfast tacos. Distances downtown are short, many top sights are free, and big theme parks sit on the edge of town for a splurge day.

Top things to do in San Antonio with kids

  1. River Walk boat cruise Ages 3+

    Go Rio boats loop the river for about 35 minutes with a guide pointing out bridges, ducks and old buildings; buy tickets online for evening slots.

  2. The Alamo Ages 6+

    Entry to the 1700s mission church is free with a timed reservation; kids enjoy the live history demonstrations and the courtyard with its huge old live oak.

  3. Mission San José Ages 5+

    The largest of the San Antonio Missions has thick stone walls, a rose window and a granary to explore, and kids can earn a free Junior Ranger badge at the visitor center.

  4. San Antonio Zoo Ages 3+

    In Brackenridge Park, with a hippo underwater viewing area, a big lorikeet aviary and a small kids zoo; tickets cost about USD 30-45 depending on the date.

  5. The DoSeum Ages 4-10

    San Antonio's children's museum has a big water table, a spy academy and climbing structures, best for an energetic half day around USD 18-22 each.

Rainy-day and cold-day options

  • Witte Museum

    Dinosaur skeletons, Texas wildlife dioramas and a hands-on body science gallery in Brackenridge Park.

  • Natural Bridge Caverns

    Guided cave walks about 30 minutes north of town, a steady cool temperature inside all year.

  • Tower of the Americas

    Ride the glass elevator up 750 feet for big city views and a 4D theatre ride at the base.

A simple first day

Morning
Visit the Alamo early before the heat and the tour groups arrive.
Afternoon
Take a Go Rio cruise, then wander the shady River Walk to La Villita craft shops.
Evening
Eat Tex-Mex at Historic Market Square and pick a sweet from a panadería counter.

Getting around

Downtown, the River Walk and the Pearl are walkable, and VIA buses cover the main routes. A car makes the missions, zoo and caverns much easier, or cycle the flat Mission Reach trail between missions.

Food kids like

Tex-Mex rules: breakfast tacos, puffy tacos, enchiladas and pan dulce, plus brisket from barbecue joints. Families eat early, around 5:30-7 pm, and portions are big enough to share.

Safety notes

  • From June to September afternoons often pass 35°C, so plan outdoor sights early, carry water and use the shady river level at midday.
  • Much of the River Walk has no railing along the water, so keep small children on the inside and hold hands in crowds.
  • Heavy storms can flood low-water road crossings quickly; never drive or walk through moving water, and tap water is safe to drink.
